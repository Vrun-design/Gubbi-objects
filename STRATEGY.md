# The Gubbi — strategy brief

*Co-founder working document. Written 16 Sep 2026, revised same day after the naming and strategy discussion. ● = decided. ◻ = open / to verify. Product lineup in §6 is NOT approved — see the note there.*

---

## 0. In one screen

**What it is.** Gubbi is a Bengaluru brand built on one idea: *the sparrow left the city, and so is everything else local.* It does two things, on two sites, under one name:

- **The Gubbi** (`thegubbi.com`) — designed objects about things worth keeping, made by Channapatna hands, sold to adults with disposable income. The brand and the business.
- **Gubbi Art Club** (`gubbiartclub.com`) — the workshops. Its own full site. The community and content engine for the objects.

**What's decided.** Objects for adults, not toys (regulation + margin). One brand, two doors, shared identity. Four domains. "The Gubbi" as the name. Every object sold puts up one sparrow nest box. Membership is a month-6 experiment, not a launch feature.

**What's not.** The product lineup. The first draft (Bengaluru icons: bus, metro, auto…) is not good enough — see §6.

**Money.** ₹5L is enough for an 8-SKU pilot of 50 each with real packaging. ₹1L/month net by month 12 ≈ 155 pieces/month. ₹3–5L/month net in year 2 needs ~470–780 pieces/month *or* corporate gifting + membership doing the heavy lifting. The constraint is artisan capacity, not demand.

## 2. Market read

### The cluster (supply)
- Channapatna: ~4,000 artisans, ~250 home units, ~50 small factories (2021). GI tag since 2005, weakly enforced; Chinese lookalikes hurt it badly.
- Artisans are poorly paid (₹150/day reported for local bead work). Their kids are leaving the craft. **Labour is the constraint, not wood.** Units say "meeting demand for new designs" is the hard part.
- The cluster already partners with NIFT/NID (e.g. Sri Beereshwara Arts & Crafts); Crafts Park trains ~300/yr. Receptive to design-led work.
- It is **lathe turnery**. Round forms are native. Boxy forms mean hybrid making. Design for the lathe.
- Roadside vendors lost ~half their revenue when the expressway opened. Motivated, under-used units exist.

### Demand
- Premium handcrafted D2C is real and growing (Ellementry, Studio Coppre, Varnam, Zwende, iTokri). None is *about a place*.
- Bengaluru-pride merch sells (Nammur has a store at 1MG). It's printed mugs and tees — proves demand, not the offer.
- Global proof of "wooden object adults buy for the desk": Candylab (US), Playforever (UK).

### The gap
> A handcrafted, designed object about a place — with a story on the box — sold at ₹1,200–3,500 to adults, made by the city's own 225-year-old craft.

Chumbak did the nearest thing in 2010 and worked, then went generic. **Specificity is the moat. Stay specific longer than feels comfortable.**

## 4. Brand strategy

### 4.1 The idea ●
**Gubbi makes objects of things worth keeping.** The name is literal: the house sparrow is disappearing from Bengaluru; so are the rain trees, the old autos, the coin phones, the hand-painted signboards, the skill of making things by hand. The objects keep them. The workshops keep the skill.

Brand line (pick one; ◻ decide before packaging print):
| Line | Kannada |
|---|---|
| **Local, before it's gone.** (recommended) | ಕಾಣೆಯಾಗೋ ಮುಂಚೆ. |
| The sparrow's still here. | ಗುಬ್ಬಿ ಇನ್ನೂ ಇದೆ. |
| Made here. Kept here. | ಇಲ್ಲೇ ಮಾಡಿದ್ದು. ಇಲ್ಲೇ ಇರ್ಲಿ. |

### 4.2 The nest-box promise ●
**Every object sold puts up one sparrow nest box in Bengaluru.** ₹100–150/unit, inside the margin. Partner with the existing *Gubbi Goodu* campaign (BCIL-ZED + Zoo Authority of Karnataka, 10,000 boxes distributed) rather than compete with it. Live counter on the site — the only social proof the brand manual allows, because it's true. Gives corporate gifting an ESG line, gives press a story, and makes the crowded "Gubbi" namespace irrelevant: you're the one *doing* the sparrow thing.

### 4.3 Names ●
The "Gubbi" namespace in Bengaluru is crowded: Gubbi Design Co. (brand-design studio, gubbi.co, @gubbi.co), Gubbi Labs (research collective), Gubbi Goodu (sparrow campaign + a resort), Gubbachhi (kids' brand), Gubbacci (tees), Gubbi town, Gubbi Veeranna. "Gubbi" alone is a place name and a surname — weak as a trademark and impossible to own online. So:

- **The Gubbi** — the objects, the brand. Said short as "Gubbi". On the box: just `gubbi` + the sparrow.
- **Gubbi Art Club** — the workshops. Keeps its name, its own full site.
- No sub-brands, no "house", no "labs", no "goodu", no "objects", no "sante". Rejected on the way here, deliberately.
- "Club" is not the name, because a club is a promise you must keep (§8). "The Gubbi" is a place; a place can do anything.

Out loud: *"We're Gubbi. We make objects about things worth keeping, and we run an art club."*

### 4.4 Shared identity ●
Same wordmark, sparrow, cobalt/paper, fonts, voice on both sites. The only difference: the descriptor under the mark (`gubbi` / *art club* vs plain `gubbi`). Art Club keeps the expressive coral-dot wordmark; The Gubbi uses the plain lockup.

Add to the brand manual:
- **Lac Red** (≈ `#c8321e`, ◻ verify against a lacquered sample) as a *product-only* colour — used on objects, never on the frame. The products carry Channapatna's palette (lac red, turmeric, indigo, green, black); the box, label and site stay cobalt on paper. Each label carries a lac swatch chip for that object.
- The brand line, in both languages.
- The nest-box counter as an approved element.

### 4.5 Voice for objects
Manual rules apply. Add one: **every object gets a ~40-word story on the box, English + spoken Kannada.** The story is the product.

## 6. Product — NOT APPROVED, rethinking

**Status: the first-draft lineup below is rejected by the founder.** "Bengaluru icons" (BMTC bus, metro, auto, Rameshwaram Cafe) reads as a souvenir line — anyone on Amazon can do icons. The lineup needs to *prove the idea in §4.1*: things worth keeping, going or gone.

### Design brief for the next draft
1. Every object must answer: *what is this keeping?* Mix three kinds — **gone** (coin phone booth, the old black-and-yellow auto livery, the BTS bus), **going** (rain tree, hand-painted signboard letters, the house sparrow, Iyengar bakery), **staying and worth it** (davara–tumbler, the rocket, the Silk Board cone as the joke that stays).
2. **Lathe-first.** Fully turnable, or turned parts + one simple cut body. No carving detail, no moving parts, no tiny parts at launch.
3. 8–14 cm. Desk scale. Ships for ₹80, not ₹250.
4. 6–8 objects, not 10. Tiers: entry ₹900–1,200 · core ₹1,400–2,200 · set ₹4,000–5,000.
5. No logos, no café names, no wordmarks. Archetypes only.
6. Each has a 40-word story, a maker's first name, a lac swatch.
7. Collection name candidate: **"Before it's gone — Vol. 1, Bengaluru."** ◻
8. Later volumes = other cities (Mysuru, Mangaluru…) — the structure must scale without renaming.

### First draft (for reference only — rejected as-is)
Auto · red bus · metro coach · davara–tumbler · Silk Board cone · rocket · Garden City trees (set of 3) · sparrow · vehicles trio set. Of these, the davara, cone, rocket, trees and sparrow are lathe-perfect and may survive; the vehicles are the weakest fit for both the lathe and the idea.

### Packaging (half the product)
One kraft/paper box family, cobalt one-colour print, sleeve with the story, Kannada–English name card, "made by" card, lac swatch chip, nest-box card ("this object put up box no. ___"). Target ₹90–140 landed. Do not skimp here.

## 7. Making

### Regulation
- **Toys (QCO) 2020**: BIS/ISI mandatory for toys for under-14s (IS 9873). Exemptions exist for DC(Handicrafts)-registered artisans and GI products — but attach to the artisan/product, not to a brand that rebrands. Too grey to rely on.
- ● **Launch as decor objects for adults.** Every box: *"Decorative object. Not a toy. Not for children under 14."* Never market to parents. A kids line, if ever, is a year-2 decision with a licensed partner or own licence (₹2–4L, 4–6 months). IS 9873 Part 1: 2025 replaces the 2019 version fully after 22 Mar 2027.
- Lac and dyes: ask each unit what they actually use. Don't claim "natural" unless it's true.

### Units (weeks 1–3)
- Three Channapatna visits, not one. Visit 1: Crafts Park + 5–6 units with sketches. Visit 2: drawings + ₹5k deposit at 2–3 units for prototypes. Visit 3: collect, compare, pick two.
- **Two units.** Small steady monthly orders beat a big one-off. Pay 20–30% above roadside rate: you're buying loyalty and first dibs on the cluster's scarcest resource.
- Spec sheet per SKU: dimensions ±3 mm, colours by lac sample, finish, reject criteria. Variance is a feature on the label, a tolerance on the spec.
- Ask: lead time per 50, Dasara/Diwali availability, wood source (hale mara), maker's mark.

### QC & stock
100% inspection for batch 1; reject target <8%. Rejects → workshop blanks or pop-up seconds. Ship via aggregator; Porter/Dunzo for same-day Bengaluru.

## 8. Business model

### Unit economics (₹1,800 object, D2C)
| | ₹ | % |
|---|---|---|
| Net of GST (◻ 12%) | ~1,607 | 100 |
| COGS | 400 | 25 |
| Packaging | 120 | 7.5 |
| Nest box | 120 | 7.5 |
| Shipping + gateway | 100 | 6 |
| Marketing | 160 | 10 |
| Damage/returns | 30 | 2 |
| **Contribution** | **~680** | **~42** |
| **Net after fixed costs** | | **~32–38** |

Plan on ~35% net. 50% only if marketing is near-zero and shipping is charged.

### Three revenue engines, in order
1. **Retail D2C** — numbered drops. Launch engine.
2. **Corporate gifting** — from month 2. "Welcome to Bengaluru" set of 3 at ₹3,900 wholesale, 100 minimum, custom sleeve. One HR person = ₹3.9L PO. This is what makes year 2 real. Nest-box line goes in the ESG paragraph.
3. **Membership — month-6 experiment, not a launch feature.** Nobody pays for "membership"; they prepay for four objects, cheaper, first, numbered:

| | One at a time | Member (₹6,000/yr) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 objects | ₹7,200 | ₹6,000 (₹1,500 each) |
| Number | whatever's left | same low number every drop |
| Access | drop day | 48 h early |
| Members-only object | — | one a year, never retail |
| Art Club Saturday | ₹1,500 | one included |
| Nest box | 1 per object | 1 per object, name on the box |

   Value ≈ ₹8,700 for ₹6,000. Sequence: sell Vol. 1 retail → month 4–6 email *buyers only* → if ≥30% convert, run it; if ~5%, drop it and stay retail + corporate. It only works if objects are *collected*, not *gifted* — Vol. 1 data decides. 300 members = ₹18L/yr prepaid, which funds the lathe without guessing.

## 10. Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Artisan capacity / lead times | High | Two units, small steady orders, lathe-first design, order batch 2 before batch 1 sells out |
| Lineup still reads as souvenir | Medium | §6 brief: every object must name what it keeps. Kill anything that's just an icon |
| Copycats within 6 months | High | Can't stop it. Win on packaging, story, photography, the nest-box promise. Design-register the heroes |
| "₹1,800 for a wooden toy?" | Medium | Never say toy. Show the box, the number, the story. Sell sets |
| Gift, not collection → no repeat | Medium | Corporate gifting is built for gifts; membership only if Vol. 1 data shows collecting |
| Name confusion with Gubbi Design Co. etc. | Medium | Composite name + device mark; own the sparrow *action*, not just the word |
| BIS enforcement | Low | Label. Don't market to parents |
| Founder attention split (objects + club + job) | High | Objects get 60% of your time for two quarters; Art Club runs on its existing rhythm |

## 11. Licensing horizon
Anime/superhero IP needs credibility you don't have yet. Two years of The Gubbi is the credibility. Start with **Indian IP** when ready — Kannada cinema, Amar Chitra Katha, Tinkle, ISRO's official merchandise programme — where a small local brand is attractive, not noise. Don't design for it now; don't design against it (turned bodies, swappable colourways, one box family).
